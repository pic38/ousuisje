Nature of the service

WhereAmI is a free tool that displays location data calculated by the user's browser or device operating system. On request, it also provides: address lookup by reverse geocoding (OpenStreetMap Nominatim), altitude comparison (IGN Géoplateforme and Open-Elevation), a search for nearby points of interest — including drinking water, toilets and cell towers, computable offline from downloaded datasets (OpenStreetMap Overpass API, ANFR) — a precipitation radar (RainViewer), and department/commune identification from downloaded IGN boundaries. Features that rely on France-specific data (cell towers, department/commune, IGN altitude) only appear when the user's position is detected in France. WhereAmI is also distributed as a standalone Android app, under the same conditions.

Limitation of liability

The information displayed (coordinates, altitude, speed, heading, accuracy, address, postcode, town, department, region, country, compared altitude, points of interest) is provided for informational purposes only, with no guarantee of accuracy, completeness, or currency.

  • Position accuracy depends on the device's sensors, network coverage, the browser used, and environmental conditions, and can range from a few metres to several hundred metres.
  • Address and points-of-interest data come from OpenStreetMap and its volunteer contributors; they may be incomplete, incorrect, or outdated in some areas.
  • Compared altitude comes from third-party digital terrain models (IGN RGE ALTI, Open-Elevation/SRTM), whose resolution and accuracy vary by location; it may differ from actual altitude.
  • Datasets downloaded for offline use (department/commune boundaries, ANFR cell towers, drinking water, toilets) are snapshots frozen at the time they were made available; they do not necessarily reflect later updates to the original sources.
  • The precipitation radar (RainViewer) is a short-term forecast, provided for informational purposes; it does not replace official weather bulletins.
  • WhereAmI should not be used as the sole source for medical, safety, legal, or emergency decisions (for example, to contact emergency services, which have their own means of locating you).
